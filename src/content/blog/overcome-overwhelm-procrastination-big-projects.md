## Understanding Overwhelm and Procrastination

### Are You Overwhelmed or Just Procrastinating?

When a project feels huge, it’s common for your brain to go completely offline. You might find yourself avoiding the task, doing anything else but working on it. This is often called procrastination, but sometimes it’s actually overwhelm.

It’s important to know that overwhelm and procrastination are different, and they require different approaches to overcome.

### Why Overwhelm Happens

Thinking about the entire project at once can push your brain into maximum stress mode. Your mind offers any other activity just to avoid the discomfort. This stress makes it hard to even start.

## Why You Don’t Need a Perfect Plan Before Starting

Trying to build a perfect, step-by-step plan before you begin can actually increase your overwhelm. You don’t need to understand the whole project before starting because clarity comes after movement, not before.

## A Simple Strategy to Break Through Overwhelm

### Step 1: Dump Everything on Paper

Spend 15 minutes writing down every single part of the project that comes to mind. Don’t organize or evaluate—just get everything out of your head and onto the page. Include all tasks, questions, and pieces you’re aware of.

### Step 2: Identify Three Relief-Providing Components

Look at what you wrote and find three parts of the project that, if done first, would reduce your sense of overwhelm the most. These might not be the first logical steps, but they are the ones that bring relief.

### Step 3: Choose One 30-Minute Action for Each

For each of those three components, pick one specific action that takes about 30 minutes to complete. Avoid choosing a whole component—focus on a single, manageable task.

### Step 4: Schedule Your Actions

Put one of those 30-minute actions into your calendar for the next couple of days. The key is to create momentum by moving forward on any piece, no matter which one.

## The Power of Momentum

Direction comes after momentum, not before. By moving on any part of your project, you’ll start to see what becomes clear and manageable. This approach can transform how productive you feel and improve your day-to-day experience with big projects.
