## What is ADHD Anger Flash?

If you're a woman with ADHD, you might recognize how quickly anger can escalate from zero to a hundred. This intense emotional response is called an ADHD anger flash. It happens when your brain bypasses the usual processing steps and goes straight to full-blown intensity.

### Not a Character Flaw

It's important to understand that this reaction is not a character flaw but a neurological feature. It can feel like a betrayal when you say something sharp or shut down, especially during sensitive times like perimenopause or before your period.

## Why Does This Happen?

Your brain's neurochemicals trigger this rapid anger response. Because it bypasses the usual emotional processing, the intensity can feel overwhelming and sometimes embarrassing once it fades.

### The Role of Neurochemicals

These neurochemicals are designed to be burned off through physical movement. Without that release, the anger can linger or cause more damage if acted upon impulsively.

## How to Address ADHD Anger Flash

The key to managing this intense anger is to avoid reacting immediately. Here’s what you can do:

### Step Away and Move

Walk out of the room or away from the person who triggered you. Engage in physical activity like an intense walk, aggressive stretches, or any movement that helps you metabolize the neurochemicals.

### Sit With the Anger

Set a timer for about 10 minutes and allow yourself to experience the anger without acting on it. Watch it peak and then fade naturally.

## Repairing After the Anger Passes

Once the intensity has lessened, reflect on what actually happened—not your interpretation or emotional reaction. If you said something reactive, keep your apology simple and direct.

### A Simple Apology

For example, say: "I was reactive earlier. That was not fair. I am sorry." This is enough. You don’t need to explain your entire neurological process.

## Final Thoughts

Remember, ADHD anger flash is a neurological response, not a personal failing. By stepping away, moving your body, and allowing the anger to pass before responding, you can protect your relationships and honor your emotional experience with kindness and understanding.
