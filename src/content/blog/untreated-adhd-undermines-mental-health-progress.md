\## Understanding the Impact of Untreated ADHD



If you've been in therapy for anxiety, depression, or trauma but still find yourself struggling, especially with productivity, you might be facing an underlying challenge: untreated ADHD. Many women I work with discover their ADHD significantly later than they wish they had. This late discovery often means they've spent years treating other conditions without addressing the root cause.



\### The Hidden Challenge



Even with therapy, you might miss appointments, forget to take medication, or find it hard to implement strategies in your business or daily life. You might feel like you work harder than anyone else you know but make less progress. This experience is common when ADHD is untreated alongside other conditions.



\## Why ADHD Undermines Other Treatments



ADHD affects executive function — the part of your brain that helps carry out decisions and strategies in real life. So, even if you make genuine progress in therapy, untreated ADHD can quietly dismantle it by making it hard to remember or follow through on what you've learned.



\### The Importance of Treating ADHD First



You might have been treated for the wrong thing first, and that’s not your fault. It’s often a gap in how mental health care is structured. Addressing ADHD first can change everything. Once ADHD is treated, progress in anxiety, depression, or trauma therapy becomes more achievable.



\## Taking Action: What You Can Do Now



At your next appointment, consider asking for a comprehensive ADHD screening. You can say you want to understand how ADHD might be affecting your ability to benefit from your current treatment. You are absolutely allowed to ask — and this step might shift your entire treatment journey.



\### Moving Forward with Support



Once ADHD is addressed, you can begin to make real progress not only in therapy but also in your business, relationships, and everyday life with partners, friends, and children. Don’t wait until you’re 60 — take action as soon as possible.



\## Final Thoughts



Remember, it’s okay to ask questions and advocate for your health. Sometimes, the right diagnosis and treatment order can make all the difference. Good luck on your journey — you deserve to thrive.
