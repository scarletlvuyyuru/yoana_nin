## The Hidden Struggle of Entrepreneurs with ADHD in Corporate Jobs

Many entrepreneurs with ADHD seem successful on the outside but are actually very miserable inside. If you find yourself working in corporate tech, finance, or similar fields and feel like you’re one inconvenience away from quitting everything, you’re not alone.

### The Challenge of Overstimulation

You might be fast, creative, and the kind of person corporate environments love. However, this often comes at a cost: overstimulation. The constant demands and long meetings can push your brain to the edge. For example, while a focused 5 to 10-minute meeting might be manageable, sitting through a 45-minute meeting can feel unbearable and make your brain want to explode.

### Everyday Life Feels Overwhelming

It’s not just work. Many people with ADHD, especially women I work with, struggle with everyday tasks like cleaning the house or cooking. Their closets might be messy, not because they’re lazy, but because their brain simply can’t handle more. This is a sign that something needs to change.

## Why You Need to Stop Working Corporate

If you recognize these signs in yourself, it might be time to stop working corporate. Your brain needs space and grace. Continuing in a high-stimulation corporate environment can lead to burnout and misery.

### Stand Up for Yourself

Give yourself permission to say no to everything that drains your energy. This might mean quitting your current job and exploring entrepreneurship or other paths that allow you to work in ways that suit your ADHD brain.

### Find Your Ground and Recharge

Consider spending more time in nature or doing activities that help you recharge. Entrepreneurship can offer the flexibility and creative freedom your brain craves, helping you thrive instead of just survive.

## Giving Grace to Yourself and Loved Ones

If you or someone you love has ADHD and is working in corporate, it’s important to offer grace and understanding. Recognize that the struggles aren’t about laziness or lack of effort but about managing a brain wired differently.

### Learning to Say No

Learning to say no to draining commitments is a powerful step toward protecting your mental health and energy. It’s okay to prioritize your well-being over societal expectations of success.

### Moving Forward with Compassion

Remember, success isn’t just about looking good on the outside. It’s about feeling good inside. If corporate life isn’t working for you, it’s okay to explore new paths that honor your unique brain and needs.
