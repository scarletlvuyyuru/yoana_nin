## Understanding the Hidden Struggles of Entrepreneurs with ADHD

Many entrepreneurs with ADHD look very successful on the outside but are actually feeling very miserable inside. This is especially true for those working in corporate tech, finance, or similar fields. You might feel like you’re one small inconvenience away from quitting everything.

### Why Corporate Life Can Be Overwhelming

You are fast, creative, and bring everything corporate loves. However, the overstimulation in these environments can push you to the edge of crashing. Long meetings, constant demands, and the structure of corporate life often don’t fit well with how your brain works.

### The Daily Challenges Beyond Work

It’s not just work that feels overwhelming. Many people with ADHD, especially women I work with, struggle with everyday tasks like cleaning the house or cooking. Their closets might be messy, but it’s not because they’re lazy—it’s because they simply cannot handle more.

## How the ADHD Brain Experiences Corporate Meetings

The ADHD brain can handle short, focused meetings—say five or ten minutes—but sitting through a 45-minute meeting can feel unbearable. You might feel like you’re going to explode because your brain just can’t focus for that long.

### Giving Yourself Grace and Space

If you or a loved one is navigating corporate life with ADHD, it’s important to give yourself grace. Recognize that these challenges are not due to a lack of effort or willpower, but because your brain needs different conditions to thrive.

## Taking Action: Standing Up for Yourself

Maybe it’s time to consider quitting what you’re doing and doing something for yourself. This could mean exploring entrepreneurship, spending more time in nature, or learning to say no to everything that drains your energy.

### Embracing Entrepreneurship and Self-Care

Entrepreneurship can offer the flexibility and creative freedom that suits your ADHD brain better. By stepping away from corporate demands, you can create a life that honors your unique needs and strengths.

Remember, your well-being matters. Standing up for yourself and making changes that protect your energy is not just okay—it’s necessary.
